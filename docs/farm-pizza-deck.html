01 The problem

You are renting your own customers.

  • The commissionJust Eat's delivery-order rate for UK partners sits around 14%, plus VAT on top of that. It comes off every order, on the food and the delivery charge, every week, forever. It does not fall when you get busier.
  • The listThe customer ordered your pizza, but the customer is theirs. You cannot text last month's regulars. You cannot see who used to order every Friday and stopped six weeks ago.
  • The shelfYour shop sits between two other pizza places on their page, sorted by their rules. Next time that customer is hungry they open the app, not your website.

Every order on there you pay for twice. Once in commission — and again by never being able to reach that customer directly.

None of this is an argument for switching the aggregator off. It is an argument for owning the orders you can own, and letting the aggregator be the shop window it is good at being.

Do not slag off Just Eat. He will have heard it and he still needs them for discovery. The line is: keep them for new faces, take the repeat trade direct.

Ask here, and write the answer down: "Roughly how many delivery orders a week come through Just Eat, and what's a typical basket?" You need those two numbers for the next slide — the figures on it are placeholders until he gives you his.

11 Did it work?

Measured, not guessed at.

  • Every textcosts 4p, recorded against that message as it goes — not estimated at the end of the month. A message sent with an offer code on it can also be measured.
  • When an order uses that codethe order's value is stamped back onto the exact message that carried it, for that customer.
  • The screen then showsfor each rule and each campaign: how many were sent, how many turned into orders, what it cost, and what came back.

Worked example: 200 win-back texts cost £8.00. Twelve come back at £24 — £288 of trade. COMEBACK15 is 15% off, so £3.60 goes back on each: £43.20 of discount. £236.80 of trade for £8.00 of texts. That is the sum the screen does for you, with your real numbers.

What these numbers are not

Revenue is the whole order, not your margin. The food still has to be made.

Someone who would have ordered anyway still counts as marketing revenue. Treat it as a strong signal, not gospel.

The “commission saved” tile applies a flat 14% to the month's direct orders. It is an estimate, and the screen says so on its face.

A message with no code cannot be measured. The thank-you rule is deliberately one of those.

The caveats box is the trust play. Any other supplier would show him the big green number and shut up. Reading the caveats out loud is what buys you the benefit of the doubt on everything else.

If he pushes on the commission-saved tile: it is the same 14% from slide 3, applied to direct orders that month. Useful as a running tally, not something to put in the accounts.
