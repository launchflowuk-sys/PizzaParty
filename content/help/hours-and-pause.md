One screen, two jobs: the week's opening times at the bottom, and the pause control at the top. The pill beside the shop name tells you which state you are in right now — **open**, **paused** or **closed**.

## Changing opening times

A grid, Monday to Sunday, with an opening time and a closing time on each row. Change what you need and press **Save hours**.

- **Leave both boxes blank for a closed day.**
- **Closing after midnight is fine.** Grays runs 11:00 to 02:00 most nights and 11:00 to 03:00 on Friday and Saturday, and that is exactly how it should be typed. The system understands that the closing time is the next morning.
- Changes are live on the website the moment you save.

Saving rewrites the whole week, not just the row you touched — so always look at the grid afterwards and check all seven days say what you meant.

## You cannot split a day

**One opening and one closing per day. That is all this screen can hold.**

If you shut between lunch and dinner — say 11:00 to 14:00, then 17:00 to 23:00 — there is no way to say so here. You would have to put the full range in and pause by hand over the afternoon, every day.

If your shop genuinely trades in two sittings, ring LaunchFlow. It needs a settings change, not a workaround.

## A typo saves as closed

This one bites, so read it twice.

**When you press Save hours, any day whose times are not both complete and valid is dropped — and a dropped day means closed.** Nothing turns red, nothing warns you, and the page comes back looking normal.

The usual way it happens: you clear a time box meaning to retype it, get distracted, and save. That day is now closed on the website and nothing on the screen shouts about it.

So, every time:

1. Press **Save hours**.
2. **Read the grid back, all seven rows.**
3. Any day that has gone blank was not saved. Type it again and save again.

## Pausing

The pause control sits at the top right of the shop's card:

1. Open the dropdown and choose **Pause 15 min**, **Pause 30 min**, **Pause 1 hour** or **Pause today**. **The dropdown starts on Resume**, so if you type a reason and press Apply without changing it, you have not paused anything — you have cleared the pause.
2. Type something into **Reason (shown to customers)**. Customers read this, so write it for them — *Back in 20 minutes, we are catching up*, or *Card payments are down, please ring us on {phone}*.
3. Press **Apply**.

New orders stop straight away and the pill beside the shop name turns to **paused**.

To start again: choose **Resume** in the same dropdown and press **Apply**.

**Pause today is 24 hours from the moment you press it, not until closing time.** Press it at 6pm and it lifts itself at 6pm tomorrow — which is the middle of your Saturday. If you mean to shut for the rest of tonight only, use it and then Resume in the morning, or better, change the day's hours.

This is also the place to pause when the reason matters. The **Pause…** dropdown on the kitchen screen is faster on the tablet, but it always tells customers "Busy" and gives you nowhere to type. If you want them to read anything else, pause from here.

## What a pause does not do

- **It does not cancel orders already in.** Everything on the kitchen board still has to be cooked and sent. Check the board.
- **Customers can still book a pre-order slot for later.** A pause stops orders for *now*, not orders for 8 o'clock.
- **It does not tell anyone.** No message goes out to customers who were part-way through ordering.
- **It does not change your opening times.** The moment it runs out, you are taking orders again.

## Nothing reminds you a pause is on

There is no alarm and no email. A pause shows in three quiet places and nowhere else:

- the pill beside the shop name on this screen;
- the shop's line in the kitchen screen header, which reads *paused until 19:45*;
- the word **Paused** in the top right of the dashboard, in the same small grey text that otherwise says *Taking orders*.

None of them will catch your eye if you are not looking. If somebody pauses at the end of a shift and goes home, the shop stays paused until the clock runs out and nobody is told.

Make it a habit: **whenever you pause, decide who is going to resume it.**

## Bank holidays

**There are no one-off closure dates.** You cannot say "closed Christmas Day" and leave the rest of the week alone.

The only way to shut for a specific day is to clear that day's times, save, and then **put them back afterwards**. Write yourself a note to do it, because nothing else will remind you — the day will stay closed every week from then on.

One last thing worth knowing about this screen: opening hours are among the things rebuilt from your shop's settings file whenever the software is updated. If a time you changed comes back to the old one, that is why. Make the change again, and tell LaunchFlow so they write it into the file properly.
