## You will know because

The New column keeps filling, the timers on the tickets have gone red, and you are accepting orders you have no chance of cooking in the time the customer is being promised.

## Do this now

1. **Buy yourself time first.** On the kitchen header, open the **Pause…** dropdown and choose **15 min** or **30 min**. Fifteen minutes of no new orders is worth more than four more tickets you cannot cook. Customers are shown the fixed reason **"Busy"** when you pause from here; if they need to read something else, pause from **Hours & pause** instead.
2. **Then tell the truth on every ticket you accept.** On a New ticket, the dropdown to the left of **Accept** is the time the customer gets told — 10 to 90 minutes. It starts on your standard collection or delivery time from **Delivery zones** and **it never moves on its own**. Change it before you press **Accept**. It is the only honest lever you have.
3. **Keep the board moving** so the timers mean something: **Cooking**, then **Ready**, then **Out for delivery** or **Collected**.
4. **Take pressure off the kitchen.** Mark the slowest items **Mark sold out** on **Menu & pricing** — anything hand-stretched, anything that ties up a fryer. **Only a manager can open Menu & pricing.** A shift lead, kitchen hand, driver or front of house will not see the screen at all, so get the manager to do it.
5. **If a ticket is genuinely impossible,** use the **Reject…** dropdown on it and choose **Too busy**, then confirm the *"Reject #…?"* box. A card order that has already been paid for is refunded in full, automatically. Better an honest rejection at five past eight than a cold pizza at ten.
6. **Do not send anything tonight.** No campaign on **Campaigns**, and do not press **Turn on** against an automation on **Marketing**. You will bring on the exact rush you are trying to get out of.
7. **When you catch up, press Resume** — on the kitchen header, or on **Hours & pause**.

## What the system does and does not do

The time a customer is promised is **a fixed number of minutes from your settings** — the collection or delivery time on **Delivery zones**, and nothing else. A far-out band's extra minutes are in the estimate the website quotes, but they are **not** in the number the ticket offers you, so a long run needs putting up by hand. It has **no idea how busy you are**. The fortieth order of the night is promised the same wait as the first unless a person changes it on Accept.

Nothing queues, throttles, spaces out or caps orders. There is no "maximum orders per fifteen minutes" anywhere.

A pause stops **everything** — you cannot pause delivery and keep collection, or the other way round.

Customers can still book a **pre-order slot** for later while you are paused, so orders for later in the evening keep landing.

The counter on each ticket turns red once it is past twenty minutes old. That is a warning to you, not something the customer sees.

## If it is still wrong

If orders keep arriving after you paused, ring LaunchFlow with the time you paused and the number of an order that came in afterwards.

## Afterwards

**Put the sold-out items back on.** Nothing clears them overnight — an item you took off at nine on Friday is still off on Saturday lunchtime. The small line above the heading on **Menu & pricing** counts them for you, in red: *"N sold out"*. Check it at the start of every shift.

**Clear the pause.** Nothing reminds you it is on except the pill on **Hours & pause** and the status on the kitchen header.
